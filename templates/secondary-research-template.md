## 3.4 Source Selection Principles

Prefer sources that are:

- Relevant to the Research Questions
- Credible and traceable
- Recent enough for the topic
- Clear about methodology or origin of data
- Independent where independent validation matters
- Based on original evidence where possible

> Multiple articles repeating the same original source should not be treated as multiple independent pieces of evidence.

# 05 — Evidence Capture & Classification

Capture evidence before interpretation.

## 5.1 Evidence Classification

### First-party / Official Evidence
Information published by the organization, company, platform, or institution that owns or operates the subject being studied.

Examples:
- Official website
- Annual report
- Help Center
- Product documentation
- Government statistics

Useful for understanding:

> What exists / What the organization reports

Do not automatically treat first-party claims as independent validation.

---

### Independent Evidence
Information from external sources that do not own the subject being studied.

Examples:
- Independent journalism
- Industry analysis
- NGO reports
- Third-party research organizations

Useful for:

> External observation / Independent assessment

---

### Academic Evidence
Research or empirical studies from academic sources.

Examples:
- Peer-reviewed papers
- Conference papers
- University research
- Empirical studies

Useful for:

> Established findings / Empirical evidence / Theory

---

### Internal Evidence
Existing evidence from within the organization.

Examples:
- Previous UX Research
- Analytics
- Customer feedback
- Internal reports
- Business data

Always retain the original context and limitations.

---

### Researcher Interpretation
Interpretation created by connecting, comparing, or synthesizing evidence.

Examples:
- Pattern
- Implication
- Design implication
- Strategic interpretation
- Opportunity

> Researcher Interpretation is not external evidence.

# 12 — Traceability & Final Audit

## 12.1 Traceability Checklist

- [ ] Every important claim can trace to a Source.
- [ ] Source and Researcher Interpretation are distinguishable.
- [ ] First-party claims are not presented as independent validation.
- [ ] Evidence can trace through Source ID → Evidence ID → Theme → Insight.
- [ ] Source quality and limitations are recorded.
- [ ] Multiple articles citing the same original source are not treated as independent evidence.
- [ ] Contradicting evidence is preserved.
- [ ] Contextual differences are preserved.
- [ ] Evidence Strength is defensible.
- [ ] Insight does not overclaim the evidence.
- [ ] Recommendation is distinguishable from Insight.
- [ ] External evidence is not automatically treated as validated local user evidence.
- [ ] Important evidence gaps are documented.

## 12.2 Source Audit

- [ ] Important claims have traceable sources.
- [ ] Source dates are appropriate for the topic.
- [ ] Source credibility has been considered.
- [ ] Original sources are preferred over secondary citation chains.
- [ ] First-party and independent evidence are clearly distinguished.

## 12.3 Evidence Audit

- [ ] Evidence and interpretation are separated.
- [ ] Counter-evidence is preserved.
- [ ] Context and limitations are visible.
- [ ] No unsupported conclusion has been introduced.

## 12.4 Synthesis Audit

- [ ] Patterns are supported by multiple pieces of evidence where appropriate.
- [ ] Cross-source synthesis does not overgeneralize.
- [ ] Evidence Strength reflects evidence quality rather than importance.
- [ ] Findings answer the Research Questions.

## 12.5 Recommendation Audit

- [ ] Recommendations trace to findings.
- [ ] Recommendations do not imply that external evidence validates our users.
- [ ] Opportunity areas are distinguishable from confirmed needs.
- [ ] Validation gaps are explicit.

# Golden Rules

1. **Source ≠ Evidence ≠ Insight ≠ Recommendation ≠ Solution.**
2. A credible source can still contain a claim that requires qualification.
3. First-party evidence should not be presented as independent validation.
4. Multiple articles citing the same study do not equal multiple independent evidence points.
5. Capture evidence before interpreting it.
6. Preserve conflicting and contextual evidence.
7. Do not generalize findings beyond the contexts supported by the evidence.
8. Secondary Research can identify patterns and opportunities; it does not automatically validate needs in our users.
9. Researcher Interpretation must always be distinguishable from external evidence.
10. Evidence Strength reflects defensibility, not business importance.
11. A validated pattern does not automatically validate a proposed solution.
12. Use evidence gaps to determine what Primary Research should happen next.
13. Maintain traceability:

**Source → Evidence → Pattern → Insight → Recommendation → Validation Gap → Decision**
